SYNTHETIC ARCHIVE POLICY AND APPLICATION NOTE

1. A member may consult public records on weekdays from 09:00 to 17:00.
2. Original documents additionally require a supervised appointment. A supervisor may expressly approve an appointment outside the weekday hours in clause 1; this approval changes only the hours condition.
3. A document marked restricted additionally requires its holder's written consent. An emergency inspection order signed by the archivist can replace that consent for inspection only. Such an order never permits reproduction.
4. A digital copy is a different format of the same record. Digital access does not by itself remove a restriction or grant reproduction permission. Reproduction of any restricted record requires the holder's separate written reproduction permission.
5. Exceptions replace only the conditions they name; all other applicable conditions remain in force. A supervised appointment by itself is not an emergency inspection order.

Case A. A member has a supervised Sunday appointment expressly approved for that time. The record is a restricted original. The holder supplied no consent of either kind. The archivist signed an emergency inspection order. May the member inspect? May the member photograph it?

Case B. The same member instead requests a digital copy on Tuesday at 11:00. The record remains restricted, there is no emergency order for this request, and there is no holder consent. The member argues that digital format makes it a public record and that the earlier appointment is sufficient. Does the policy permit this access? Which conditions matter?

Task: explain both outcomes from the clauses, including why an exception to one condition does not waive an unrelated condition. Do not import external law.
