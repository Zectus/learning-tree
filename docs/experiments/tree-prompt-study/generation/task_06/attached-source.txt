SYNTHETIC MATHEMATICAL EXCERPT: Two positive assay reports

A population contains 20,000 people, 2% with condition D. Each of two assays has P(+|D)=0.90 and P(+|not D)=0.05. The two assay outcomes are independent CONDITIONAL ON D and independent CONDITIONAL ON not D. These conditional assumptions are supplied properties of the model, not claims of unconditional independence.

On that model, expected counts among the people positive on both assays are 400*(0.90)^2=324 with D and 19,600*(0.05)^2=49 without D. Thus P(D|++)=324/(324+49), approximately 86.9%. These are expected counts in the model; no guarantee of exactly these counts in a finite realized sample is claimed.

Contrast an apparent second test that simply copies the first report, rather than generating a new conditionally independent outcome. Among first-positive reports there are expected counts 400*0.90=360 with D and 19,600*0.05=980 without D. A copied second positive leaves the fraction 360/(360+980), approximately 26.9%, unchanged. It is incorrect to square the within-group positive fractions for a copied report.

The source's claim is an inference about the group distribution, not certainty about any single person. Explain how the conditioning and the actual generation of the second report license each calculation.
