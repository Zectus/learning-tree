DOCUMENT STRUCTURE

Organize sections around coherent learning steps in the subject. An example, a definition, and a worked use may belong together when they develop the same idea; a new difficulty may need a separate section. There is no section quota. Merge repetitive treatments and give the central idea more attention than peripheral cases.

Begin with a concrete question or situation that makes the idea worth understanding. A brief statement of purpose is welcome when it helps the reader know what question is being answered. Avoid announcements of the document's outline and repeated motivational preambles.

Build meaning through an accessible case, then introduce the general statement and its notation. When the result is to be derived, let the calculation or argument produce it before displaying it as a finished rule. A brief statement of the target is fine; do not replace its development with announcing it and checking one example. Explain each new symbol's role and how to read the whole expression. Explain what unfamiliar operations do and how conventions affect the expression's meaning. Define suppressed labels and graph aliases when their meaning matters. A display, conventional-looking superscript, or suggestive name is not an explanation. Use established prerequisite notation economically. Translate abstract phrases into concrete comparisons, choices, or numerical requirements before relying on them. Metaphors should help without implying properties the concept does not have. Use a contrasting case when it resolves a likely misconception.

Show how the reader uses the idea at this node's level: interpreting information, making a prediction, distinguishing cases, or carrying out a method. A conceptual node still needs worked use even when calculation techniques are excluded. Keep the example's purpose visible and its background requirements modest.

When a method represents a physical or information-changing operation, distinguish the operation from the data it reports. State what is done, what information is retained or discarded, and the model that licenses the resulting state or subsequent prediction. Equal reported statistics do not by themselves establish equal procedures or updates; concealing a finer record need not be equivalent to carrying out a coarser operation. In a multi-stage calculation, show what enters each stage after any conditioning or selection and state the intervening changes assumed by the model. Clarify the worked setup without accumulating peripheral caveats.

When interpreting a probability or statistic, identify whether it concerns an individual event, a distribution over repeated preparations, or an estimate from finite data. Different distributions need not allow certain identification from one event. A spread is not automatically a maximum deviation, an instrument's resolution, or the uncertainty of an estimated average. Do not infer certainty, a bound, or an inverse identification unless the established model supports it. Explain only the distinctions relevant to this lesson, using its existing tools rather than importing a new statistical theory.

Match justification to the learning goal. Separate what is defined or supplied from what is being inferred. A definition needs meaning and a reason it serves the problem, rather than a manufactured proof. A physical prescription or a mathematical fact explicitly supplied by the scope may be stated as an input. A central consequence of those inputs needs a visible connecting argument. Accept permitted inputs explicitly, then show how they lead to the new result. If a useful choice must be found, begin with what is unknown and work out that choice instead of announcing it and only verifying it. Do not import unfamiliar background or re-prove prerequisite theorems.

Show the operation where understanding depends on it. Explain why a useful choice is made, display the intermediate step that does the work, and interpret what it produces. When a construction represents an observable outcome, demonstrate that prediction. Distinguish an approximation from an exact result or limit at the point where that distinction matters. Phrases such as "matching gives," "one finds," "it follows," or "as can be checked" warrant inspection: if they hide the step this node teaches, write that step; if they compress routine established work, keep them concise. A named identity or general guarantee is not justified merely by a few numerical examples. State assumptions when they are used, without burying the central idea in caveats or formalism.

When justifying an approximation, check whether the change being modeled also changes the scales used in its validity estimate. Identify the quantity being controlled and the range or history over which it must remain controlled. A small starting ratio or a small endpoint result alone does not establish validity throughout that range. Explain the relevant check with the tools available at this node's level.

Let the reader complete one understandable use of the central idea before adding several qualifications, representations, or cases. Introduce notation and cautions where they first do useful work. State a limitation clearly once; return to it only when the next task needs a new distinction. If the opening carries too many new elements, distribute them across the progression rather than shortening the reasoning that makes the idea understandable.

Introduce supporting contrasts after the central idea is secure. Do not give a catalog of exceptions more space than understanding and using the ordinary case. Keep excluded techniques and later formalism outside the lesson, while preserving motivation and accurate interpretation.

A visual must agree with the mathematical or factual object described. Do not depict a missing point by deleting a visible interval or imply that finite sampling captures all behavior. Use only supported graph features. If the format cannot faithfully mark a distinction, explain the representation's limitation and choose a different illustration rather than inventing syntax or claiming the picture shows something it does not.

Place main questions at natural pauses after their required explanation or worked instruction. Let the reader try one manageable use before accumulating several independent difficulties; do not interrupt a necessary argument or ask for an untaught result. Keep questions embedded in the reading sequence, not grouped in an exercises section, and number [QUESTION N] blocks consecutively. Keep the particular solution and option evaluation out of all visible prose and graphs. General teaching on other cases is necessary preparation, not answer leakage; a thinly renamed copy that supplies the distinguishing conclusion still leaks. After a question, continue to new content without explaining or defending that question's answer. Read the question-construction rules before drafting the first question.

After the last main question, add bonus practice questions using this separate format — genuine extra practice, not a preview of anything not yet covered. However many genuinely earn a place is up to you; there's no target count here either, could be a couple, could be quite a few. This is a good place to reach for something genuinely interesting if one comes to mind: a surprising special case, a configuration that makes the structure of the topic click in a new way, a harder problem that's satisfying to push through — using only the tools and derivations this document just built. Don't force it, though; a few solid harder versions of the main material, applied to a less standard case or a sharper edge case the main questions didn't reach, are just as good a use of a bonus slot. A bonus question may also pull in a non-obvious connection to something already established here or seen earlier in the sequence. What a bonus question must never do is require knowledge the reader hasn't been given — even though the context below tells you what later topic this one feeds into, do not write a question whose answer depends on understanding that later topic; the reader hasn't seen it yet. Do not require a new theorem, technique, convention, or physical premise just because it feels like a natural next step. Fresh reasoning and combinations of already established operations are welcome: the learner need not have seen that exact calculation or conclusion modeled, or the question would only repeat it. Trace the solution back to tools already taught here or in the prerequisites, and distinguish those tools from the new work the question intentionally leaves to the learner. Gesturing at a real-world structure or application this machinery is used for elsewhere is fine as flavor in the setup, but the question itself must be fully answerable from tools and premises established here or in the prerequisites.
