=== SECTION 1: Standard form and interior-exterior regions ===

A circle is the set of all points \(P(x, y)\) in the plane whose distance from a fixed center \(C(h, k)\) equals a fixed positive radius \(r > 0\). Translating that geometric condition directly with the planar distance formula gives
\[
\sqrt{(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2} = r.
\]
Because both sides are nonnegative, squaring produces an algebraically equivalent equation with no extraneous points:
\[
(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2.
\]
This is the standard (or center-radius) form of a circle. When the center is at the origin \((0, 0)\), it simplifies to \(x^2 + y^2 = r^2\).

[CALLOUT]
id: circle-standard
label: Formula
title: Standard form of a circle
tone: key

The circle with center \(C(h, k)\) and radius \(r > 0\) has standard equation
\[
(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2.
\]
For any point \(P(x, y)\) in the plane:
(1) \((x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 < r^2\) if and only if \(P\) lies in the interior of the circle (\(PC < r\)).
(2) \((x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2\) if and only if \(P\) lies on the circle (\(PC = r\)).
(3) \((x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 > r^2\) if and only if \(P\) lies in the exterior of the circle (\(PC > r\)).
[/CALLOUT]

Read the signs and the right-hand side carefully when extracting \(h\), \(k\), and \(r\). For example, rewrite \((x + 3)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 25\) as \((x - (-3))^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 5^2\): its center is \((-3, 2)\), not \((3, -2)\), and its radius is \(5\), not \(25\).

To see [REF: circle-standard.1 | interior] and [REF: circle-standard.3 | exterior] classification in action, take the circle \((x - 2)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 9\), centered at \(C(2, -1)\) with radius \(r = 3\).
- Test \(A(3, 0)\): \((3 - 2)^2 + (0 + 1)^2 = 1 + 1 = 2 < 9\), so \(A\) is strictly inside the circle.
- Test \(B(5, -1)\): \((5 - 2)^2 + (-1 + 1)^2 = 9 = 9\), so \(B\) lies on the circle.
- Test \(D(-1, 2)\): \((-1 - 2)^2 + (2 + 1)^2 = 9 + 9 = 18 > 9\), so \(D\) lies strictly outside the circle.

=== SECTION 2: General form, completing the square, and degeneracies ===

Expanding [REF: circle-standard | the standard equation] \((x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2\) produces
\[
x^2 + y^2 - 2hx - 2ky + (h^2 + k^2 - r^2) = 0.
\]
Multiplying through by any nonzero constant \(A \neq 0\) yields the general circle form:
\[
Ax^2 + Ay^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0 \qquad (A \neq 0).
\]
Notice its two defining structural hallmarks: \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) have equal nonzero coefficients \(A\), and there is no \(xy\)-term.

However, not every equation of the form \(Ax^2 + Ay^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0\) actually defines a circle! To find out what geometric set it describes, reverse the expansion:
1. Divide the entire equation by \(A\) so that the coefficients of \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) are both \(1\).
2. Group the \(x\)-terms and \(y\)-terms together and move the constant term to the right-hand side.
3. Complete the square in \(x\) and in \(y\), adding the exact same constants to the right-hand side to keep the equation balanced.

This always brings the equation to the form
\[
(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = R,
\]
where the sign of the resulting constant \(R\) determines the geometry completely.

[TABLE]
Value of \(R\) | Geometric locus | Example
\(R > 0\) | Real circle with center \((h, k)\) and radius \(r = \sqrt{R}\) | \((x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 8\)
\(R = 0\) | Degenerate single point \(\{(h, k)\}\) | \((x + 2)^2 + (y - 5)^2 = 0\)
\(R < 0\) | Empty locus \(\emptyset\) (no real points) | \((x - 1)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = -2\)
[/TABLE]

Let us work through all three outcomes explicitly.
- First, take \(2x^2 + 2y^2 - 12x + 8y + 10 = 0\). Divide by \(2\) first:
\[
x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y + 5 = 0 \implies (x^2 - 6x) + (y^2 + 4y) = -5.
\]
Half of \(-6\) squared is \(9\), and half of \(4\) squared is \(4\). Adding \(9 + 4\) to both sides gives
\[
(x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = -5 + 9 + 4 = 8.
\]
Since \(R = 8 > 0\), this is a real circle centered at \((3, -2)\) with radius \(\sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}\).
- Second, take \(x^2 + y^2 + 4x - 10y + 29 = 0\):
\[
(x^2 + 4x + 4) + (y^2 - 10y + 25) = -29 + 4 + 25 = 0 \implies (x + 2)^2 + (y - 5)^2 = 0.
\]
Because a sum of two squares of real numbers vanishes only when both squares are \(0\), the sole solution is the single point \((-2, 5)\).
- Third, take \(3x^2 + 3y^2 - 6x + 12y + 21 = 0\). Dividing by \(3\) gives \(x^2 + y^2 - 2x + 4y + 7 = 0\), and completing the square yields
\[
(x - 1)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = -7 + 1 + 4 = -2 < 0.
\]
No real pair \((x, y)\) can make a sum of squares negative, so the locus is empty.

[CALLOUT]
id: divide-leading-a
label: Common pitfall
title: Dividing by \(A\) before completing the square
tone: caution

When \(A \neq 1\) in \(Ax^2 + Ay^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0\), you must divide the entire equation by \(A\) before taking half of the linear coefficients to complete the square. Forgetting to divide \(F\) by \(A\) distorts the constant \(R\) and misclassifies the radius and degeneracy threshold.
[/CALLOUT]

=== SECTION 3: Constructing circle equations from geometric data ===

Because a circle is determined by three numbers—\((h, k, r)\) in standard form, or \((D, E, F)\) in normalized general form \(x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0\)—three independent geometric pieces of information pin it down uniquely.

Case 1: Center and a point on the circle. Given center \(C(h, k)\) and a point \(P(x_1, y_1)\) on the circle, compute \(r^2 = (x_1 - h)^2 + (y_1 - k)^2\) directly and write [REF: circle-standard | standard form].

Case 2: Endpoints of a diameter. Given diameter endpoints \(A(x_1, y_1)\) and \(B(x_2, y_2)\), the center \(C\) is the midpoint of \(AB\), and the radius \(r\) is the distance from \(C\) to either endpoint (equivalently, \(r = \frac{1}{2} AB\)). For instance, if a diameter joins \(A(-1, 5)\) and \(B(5, -3)\), the center is
\[
C = \left(\frac{-1 + 5}{2},\; \frac{5 + (-3)}{2}\right) = (2, 1),
\]
and \(r^2 = (5 - 2)^2 + (-3 - 1)^2 = 9 + 16 = 25\), giving \((x - 2)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = 25\).

Case 3: Three non-collinear points. Any three non-collinear points \(P, Q, R\) determine a unique circle (the circumcircle of \(\triangle PQR\)). Two reliable methods find its equation:
- Algebraic linear-system method: substitute all three points into the normalized general equation \(x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0\). Because the unknowns \(D, E, F\) appear linearly, this produces a \(3 \times 3\) linear system!
- Geometric perpendicular-bisector method: intersect the perpendicular bisectors of two chords (say \(PQ\) and \(PR\)) to locate the center \((h, k)\), then compute \(r^2\) as the squared distance to any of the three points.

Watch both methods on \(P(0, 0)\), \(Q(6, 0)\), and \(R(2, 4)\). Using the algebraic linear system:
- Plugging in \(P(0, 0)\): \(0 + 0 + 0 + 0 + F = 0 \implies F = 0\).
- Plugging in \(Q(6, 0)\): \(36 + 0 + 6D + 0 + 0 = 0 \implies D = -6\).
- Plugging in \(R(2, 4)\): \(4 + 16 + 2(-6) + 4E + 0 = 0 \implies 8 + 4E = 0 \implies E = -2\).

Thus the general equation is \(x^2 + y^2 - 6x - 2y = 0\), and completing the square yields \((x - 3)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = 10\) (center \((3, 1)\), radius \(\sqrt{10}\)). Geometrically, the perpendicular bisector of \(PQ\) is \(x = 3\), and the perpendicular bisector of \(PR\) (midpoint \((1, 2)\), slope \(2\)) is \(y - 2 = -\frac{1}{2}(x - 1)\), intersecting at the exact same center \((3, 1)\) with \(r^2 = 3^2 + 1^2 = 10\).
