=== SECTION 1: Algebraic tangent lines to conics ===

Why does a satellite dish shaped like a parabola gather faint signals from space into one tiny receiver, why can a person standing at one focus of an elliptical whispering gallery hear a whisper from the other focus across the room, and how does a Cassegrain telescope bounce light cleanly between a parabolic primary mirror and a hyperbolic secondary mirror? All three optical miracles follow from the geometry of tangent lines to conics—and we can derive every single tangent formula and reflection law purely algebraically, using nothing beyond the repeated-root (\(\Delta = 0\)) tangency criterion from line-circle intersections.

Recall what makes a line \(L\) tangent to a conic at a point \(P_0(x_0, y_0)\) on the curve: substituting the line's equation into the conic's equation produces a quadratic equation for which \(P_0\) is a DOUBLE (repeated) root (\(\Delta = 0\)). (Lines parallel to a parabola's axis or a hyperbola's asymptotes drop the degree to \(1\) and cross the curve once without being tangent.)

Start with the parabola \(y^2 = 4px\) (\(p \neq 0\)) and a point \(P_0(x_0, y_0)\) on it, so \(y_0^2 = 4px_0\). Let a line through \(P_0\) with nonzero slope \(m\) have equation \(y - y_0 = m(x - x_0)\), or \(x - x_0 = \frac{y - y_0}{m}\). Subtracting \(y_0^2 = 4px_0\) from \(y^2 = 4px\) gives
\[
y^2 - y_0^2 = 4p(x - x_0) = \frac{4p}{m}(y - y_0).
\]
Factoring out \((y - y_0)\) immediately exhibits the known intersection \(y = y_0\):
\[
(y - y_0)\left(y + y_0 - \frac{4p}{m}\right) = 0.
\]
For \(y = y_0\) to be a DOUBLE root, the second factor must ALSO vanish at \(y = y_0\):
\[
2y_0 - \frac{4p}{m} = 0 \implies m = \frac{2p}{y_0} \qquad (y_0 \neq 0).
\]
Substituting \(m = \frac{2p}{y_0}\) back into \(y - y_0 = m(x - x_0)\) gives \(y_0 y - y_0^2 = 2px - 2px_0\). Replacing \(y_0^2\) with \(4px_0\) and adding \(4px_0\) to both sides yields the remarkably symmetric tangent equation:
\[
y_0 y = 2p(x + x_0).
\]
Check the vertex \(P_0(0, 0)\): the formula becomes \(0 = 2px\), or \(x = 0\), which is indeed the vertical tangent at the vertex!

Now run the exact same algebraic double-root derivation for a point \(P_0(x_0, y_0)\) on the centered ellipse \(\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1\) (so \(\frac{x_0^2}{a^2} + \frac{y_0^2}{b^2} = 1\)). Subtracting the two equations gives
\[
\frac{x^2 - x_0^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2 - y_0^2}{b^2} = 0.
\]
For a non-vertical line \(y - y_0 = m(x - x_0)\) through \(P_0\), write \(y + y_0 = 2y_0 + m(x - x_0)\) and factor out \((x - x_0)\):
\[
(x - x_0)\left[\frac{x + x_0}{a^2} + \frac{m\bigl(2y_0 + m(x - x_0)\bigr)}{b^2}\right] = 0.
\]
For \(x = x_0\) to be a repeated root, the expression in brackets must vanish when \(x = x_0\):
\[
\frac{2x_0}{a^2} + \frac{2my_0}{b^2} = 0 \implies m = -\frac{b^2 x_0}{a^2 y_0} \qquad (y_0 \neq 0).
\]
Substituting \(m\) into \(y - y_0 = m(x - x_0)\) and dividing by \(b^2\) gives
\[
\frac{y_0 y - y_0^2}{b^2} = -\frac{x_0 x - x_0^2}{a^2} \implies \frac{x_0 x}{a^2} + \frac{y_0 y}{b^2} = \frac{x_0^2}{a^2} + \frac{y_0^2}{b^2} = 1.
\]
At the vertices \((\pm a, 0)\), this formula yields \(x = \pm a\), correctly capturing the vertical tangents as well! Replacing \(+b^2\) by \(-b^2\) throughout the exact same steps produces the tangent line to the centered hyperbola \(\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1\):
\[
\frac{x_0 x}{a^2} - \frac{y_0 y}{b^2} = 1.
\]

[CALLOUT]
id: tangent-replacement
label: Rule
title: Symmetric replacement pattern for tangent lines at a point on a conic
tone: key

When \(P_0(x_0, y_0)\) lies ON a standard centered conic, its tangent line is obtained by replacing \(x^2 \to x_0 x\), \(y^2 \to y_0 y\), \(2x \to x + x_0\), and \(2y \to y + y_0\):
(1) Parabola \(y^2 = 4px\): \(y_0 y = 2p(x + x_0)\). (Similarly, \(x^2 = 4py\) has tangent \(x_0 x = 2p(y + y_0)\).)
(2) Ellipse \(\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1\): \(\frac{x_0 x}{a^2} + \frac{y_0 y}{b^2} = 1\).
(3) Hyperbola \(\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1\): \(\frac{x_0 x}{a^2} - \frac{y_0 y}{b^2} = 1\).
[/CALLOUT]

Be careful: [REF: tangent-replacement | this replacement rule] ONLY produces the tangent line when \(P_0(x_0, y_0)\) actually satisfies the conic's equation! If you need tangent lines passing through an external point \((x_1, y_1)\) NOT on the conic, write \(y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)\), substitute into the conic, and solve \(\Delta = 0\) for the slope \(m\) (just as we did for circles).

=== SECTION 2: The optical reflective property of parabolas ===

In geometric optics, a ray of light or sound bounces off a smooth curved mirror exactly as if it hit the tangent line at the point of impact \(P_0\): the incoming ray and the reflected ray make EQUAL angles with the tangent line (equivalently, equal angles with the normal line perpendicular to the tangent).

Now test a ray traveling parallel to the axis of symmetry of the parabola \(y^2 = 4px\) (\(p > 0\)), striking the mirror at any point \(P_0(x_0, y_0)\) with \(y_0 \neq 0\). By [REF: tangent-replacement.1 | the parabola tangent equation], the tangent line at \(P_0\) is
\[
y_0 y = 2p(x + x_0).
\]
Where does this tangent line cross the \(x\)-axis? Setting \(y = 0\) gives \(x = -x_0\), so the tangent crosses the axis at \(T(-x_0, 0)\).

Now compare the two sides \(TF\) and \(FP_0\) of triangle \(TFP_0\), where \(F(p, 0)\) is the focus:
- Along the \(x\)-axis, since \(x_0 > 0\) and \(p > 0\), the distance from \(T(-x_0, 0)\) to \(F(p, 0)\) is
\[
TF = p - (-x_0) = x_0 + p.
\]
- By the parabola's own focus-directrix definition, the distance \(FP_0\) from \(P_0(x_0, y_0)\) to the focus equals its perpendicular distance to the directrix \(x = -p\), which is ALSO
\[
FP_0 = x_0 - (-p) = x_0 + p.
\]
Therefore \(TF = FP_0\), so triangle \(TFP_0\) is ISOSCELES! Its two base angles are equal: \(\angle FTP_0 = \angle FP_0 T\). Meanwhile, because the incoming horizontal ray at \(P_0\) is parallel to the \(x\)-axis \(TF\), the angle between the incoming ray and the tangent line at \(P_0\) equals the corresponding angle \(\angle FTP_0\). Hence the incoming horizontal ray and the focal segment \(P_0 F\) make EQUAL angles with the tangent line!

Every ray arriving parallel to a parabola's axis reflects directly through the focus \(F\); reversing the path, every ray emitted from a source at \(F\) reflects outward into a beam strictly parallel to the axis.

=== SECTION 3: Reflective properties of ellipses and hyperbolas ===

Do ellipses and hyperbolas satisfy equally clean focal reflection laws? Yes—and we can verify both using the Angle Bisector Theorem from Euclidean geometry, which states that a line through vertex \(P_0\) of triangle \(F_1 P_0 F_2\) meeting side \(F_1 F_2\) at \(N\) bisects \(\angle F_1 P_0 F_2\) if and only if \(\frac{F_1 N}{N F_2} = \frac{P_0 F_1}{P_0 F_2}\).

First, take any point \(P_0(x_0, y_0)\) (with \(x_0, y_0 \neq 0\)) on the ellipse \(\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1\), with foci \(F_1(-c, 0)\) and \(F_2(c, 0)\) and eccentricity \(e = \frac{c}{a}\). From [REF: tangent-replacement.2 | the ellipse tangent equation], the tangent has slope \(m_t = -\frac{b^2 x_0}{a^2 y_0}\), so the perpendicular NORMAL line at \(P_0\) has slope \(m_n = \frac{a^2 y_0}{b^2 x_0}\):
\[
y - y_0 = \frac{a^2 y_0}{b^2 x_0}(x - x_0).
\]
Set \(y = 0\) to locate the point \(N(x_N, 0)\) where the normal crosses the \(x\)-axis:
\[
-y_0 = \frac{a^2 y_0}{b^2 x_0}(x_N - x_0) \implies x_N = x_0\left(1 - \frac{b^2}{a^2}\right) = \frac{c^2}{a^2}x_0 = e^2 x_0.
\]
Since \(\lvert x_0 \rvert \le a\) and \(0 < e < 1\), we have \(\lvert e^2 x_0 \rvert < ae = c\), so \(N\) lies strictly between \(F_1\) and \(F_2\), dividing segment \(F_1 F_2\) in the ratio
\[
\frac{F_1 N}{N F_2} = \frac{e^2 x_0 - (-c)}{c - e^2 x_0} = \frac{e(a + e x_0)}{e(a - e x_0)} = \frac{a + e x_0}{a - e x_0}.
\]
Now compute the focal distances \(P_0 F_1\) and \(P_0 F_2\) directly using \(y_0^2 = b^2\bigl(1 - \frac{x_0^2}{a^2}\bigr)\) and \(a^2 - b^2 = c^2 = a^2 e^2\):
\[
P_0 F_1^2 = (x_0 + c)^2 + b^2\left(1 - \frac{x_0^2}{a^2}\right) = e^2 x_0^2 + 2aex_0 + (c^2 + b^2) = (a + ex_0)^2.
\]
Since \(a + ex_0 > 0\), we get \(P_0 F_1 = a + ex_0\), and identically \(P_0 F_2 = a - ex_0\). Thus
\[
\frac{F_1 N}{N F_2} = \frac{a + ex_0}{a - ex_0} = \frac{P_0 F_1}{P_0 F_2}.
\]
By the converse of the Angle Bisector Theorem, the normal line \(P_0 N\) BISECTS \(\angle F_1 P_0 F_2\)! Therefore, every ray emitted from one focus \(F_1\) reflects off the elliptical wall directly to the other focus \(F_2\).

Finally, apply the same ratio check to a point \(P_0(x_0, y_0)\) on the right branch (\(x_0 \ge a\)) of the hyperbola \(\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1\), with foci \(F_1(-c, 0), F_2(c, 0)\) and \(e = \frac{c}{a} > 1\). By [REF: tangent-replacement.3 | the hyperbola tangent equation], the TANGENT line \(\frac{x_0 x}{a^2} - \frac{y_0 y}{b^2} = 1\) crosses the \(x\)-axis at \(T\bigl(\frac{a^2}{x_0}, 0\bigr)\). Because \(x_0 \ge a\) and \(a < c\), we have \(0 < \frac{a^2}{x_0} \le a < c\), so \(T\) lies strictly between \(F_1\) and \(F_2\)! Computing the segment ratio on the \(x\)-axis gives
\[
\frac{F_1 T}{T F_2} = \frac{\frac{a^2}{x_0} + c}{c - \frac{a^2}{x_0}} = \frac{a^2 + c x_0}{c x_0 - a^2} = \frac{a + e x_0}{e x_0 - a}.
\]
Meanwhile, the same algebraic expansion with \(y_0^2 = b^2\bigl(\frac{x_0^2}{a^2} - 1\bigr)\) and \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\) yields \(P_0 F_1 = e x_0 + a\) and \(P_0 F_2 = e x_0 - a\), so
\[
\frac{F_1 T}{T F_2} = \frac{P_0 F_1}{P_0 F_2}.
\]
Thus for a hyperbola, it is the TANGENT line \(P_0 T\) (not the normal!) that bisects the interior angle \(\angle F_1 P_0 F_2\). Translating equal angles with the tangent into light rays yields two reverse-path versions of the hyperbolic reflection law:
- A ray emitted from focus \(F_2\) striking the concave (inner) side of its branch reflects directly AWAY from the other focus \(F_1\) (diverging as if it originated at \(F_1\)).
- Reversing the light ray, an incoming ray striking the convex (outer) side of the right branch while aimed TOWARD \(F_2\) reflects directly TO the other focus \(F_1\)!

[CALLOUT]
id: reflection-laws
label: Summary
title: Optical reflective properties of the three conics
tone: key

(1) Parabola: Rays parallel to the axis reflect through the focus \(F\); rays emitted from \(F\) reflect parallel to the axis (parabolic antennas, headlights, primary telescope mirrors).
(2) Ellipse: Rays emitted from one focus \(F_1\) reflect directly to the other focus \(F_2\) (whispering galleries, medical lithotripsy shock-wave reflectors).
(3) Hyperbola: Rays aimed toward one focus \(F_2\) from the convex side reflect directly to the other focus \(F_1\), while rays from \(F_2\) hitting the concave side reflect directly away from \(F_1\) (Cassegrain telescope secondary mirrors).
[/CALLOUT]
