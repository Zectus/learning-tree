=== SECTION 1: Velocity vectors, bearings, and moving media ===

When an airplane flies through wind or a boat crosses a flowing river, the direction the nose points is usually NOT the direction the craft actually travels over the ground. A pilot pointing due North through an eastward crosswind gets carried diagonally northeast; a swimmer aiming straight across a fast stream lands far downstream. Velocity vectors let us predict both the true path and the exact steering correction needed to stay on course.

A constant 2D velocity \(\mathbf{v}\) is a vector whose magnitude \(\|\mathbf{v}\|\) is speed (e.g., in \(\text{km/h}\) or \(\text{m/s}\)) and whose direction is the direction of travel. In planar navigation, we align East with the positive \(x\)-axis and North with the positive \(y\)-axis, and convert any three-figure compass bearing \(\beta\) (measured clockwise from due North) into its standard mathematical angle \(\theta\) (measured counterclockwise from due East) via
\[
\theta = 90^\circ - \beta \qquad (\text{adding } 360^\circ \text{ if needed so } \theta \in [0^\circ, 360^\circ)).
\]
Now model a craft moving inside a moving medium (air or water). Over one unit of time, the craft displaces by \(\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{craft}}\) relative to the surrounding fluid, while the fluid itself carries the craft along by \(\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{medium}}\) relative to the Earth. By head-to-tail vector addition, the craft's actual velocity over the Earth is the sum:
\[
\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{ground}} = \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{craft}} + \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{medium}}.
\]

[CALLOUT]
id: vel-comp
label: Principle
title: Velocity composition in a moving medium
tone: key

When a craft moves with velocity \(\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{craft}}\) relative to a medium (air or water) that itself moves with velocity \(\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{medium}}\) relative to the ground, the craft's true velocity over the ground is
\[
\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{ground}} = \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{craft}} + \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{medium}}.
\]

(1) Heading and airspeed (or water-speed) describe \(\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{craft}}\)—the direction the craft's nose points and its speed through the fluid.
(2) Ground track (or true course) and ground speed describe \(\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{ground}}\)—the actual direction and speed over the Earth.
[/CALLOUT]

[CALLOUT]
id: wind-direction-trap
label: Common pitfall
title: Distinguish where a wind blows TOWARD from where it blows FROM
tone: caution

Always check the wording of a wind or current direction. A wind blowing toward East (bearing \(090^\circ\)) has velocity vector \(\langle +w, 0 \rangle\). In weather reports, however, a "west wind" or a wind "from \(270^\circ\)" means air coming FROM the West and therefore blowing TOWARD the East (\(\langle +w, 0 \rangle\)), \(180^\circ\) opposite to its source bearing. Never insert a "from" bearing directly into \(\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{medium}}\) without reversing it by \(180^\circ\) first.
[/CALLOUT]

Let us work a forward navigation example. A light aircraft heads due North (bearing \(000^\circ\), standard angle \(90^\circ\)) at an airspeed of \(200\text{ km/h}\) while a steady wind of \(50\text{ km/h}\) blows toward bearing \(120^\circ\) (\(\text{S } 60^\circ \text{ E}\)). Find the aircraft's ground speed and true ground track.
1. Convert the wind bearing \(120^\circ\) to standard angle \(\theta_w = 90^\circ - 120^\circ = -30^\circ \equiv 330^\circ\).
2. Write both velocities in East–North component form:
\[
\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}} = \langle 0,\; 200 \rangle\text{ km/h},
\]
\[
\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{wind}} = 50\langle \cos 330^\circ,\; \sin 330^\circ \rangle = \langle 25\sqrt{3},\; -25 \rangle\text{ km/h} \approx \langle 43.30,\; -25 \rangle\text{ km/h}.
\]
3. Add using [REF: vel-comp | velocity composition]:
\[
\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{ground}} = \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{air}} + \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{wind}} = \langle 25\sqrt{3},\; 175 \rangle\text{ km/h} \approx \langle 43.30,\; 175 \rangle\text{ km/h}.
\]
4. Recover ground speed and track:
\[
\|\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{ground}}\| = \sqrt{(25\sqrt{3})^2 + 175^2} = \sqrt{1875 + 30625} = \sqrt{32500} = 50\sqrt{13} \approx 180.3\text{ km/h}.
\]
Since both components are positive (Quadrant I), the standard angle is \(\theta_g = \arctan\left(\frac{175}{25\sqrt{3}}\right) = \arctan\left(\frac{7}{\sqrt{3}}\right) \approx 76.1^\circ\), which corresponds to compass bearing \(\beta_g = 90^\circ - 76.1^\circ = 013.9^\circ\) (\(\text{N } 13.9^\circ \text{ E}\)). Even though the pilot points straight North, the aircraft drifts \(13.9^\circ\) East of North and slows to \(180.3\text{ km/h}\) over the ground.

=== SECTION 2: Drift correction and the impossibility threshold ===

In real navigation, the problem usually runs in reverse: you know the true ground track you WANT to follow (say straight across a river to a dock directly opposite, or directly toward a destination airport) and you know your craft's speed \(v_c = \|\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{craft}}\|\) and the wind or current \(\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{medium}}\). Which heading angle must you steer into the crosswind or current so that the sideways drift cancels out completely?

Let us work through a river-crossing setup. A ferry with water-speed \(v_b = 10\text{ km/h}\) wants to travel straight due North across a river whose current flows due East at speed \(v_r = 6\text{ km/h}\) (\(\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{river}} = \langle 6, 0 \rangle\)). To prevent being swept East, the captain must angle the bow WEST of North by an unknown crab angle \(\phi \in (0^\circ, 90^\circ)\) (compass bearing \(360^\circ - \phi\), standard angle \(90^\circ + \phi\)). In components:
\[
\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{boat}} = \langle -v_b\sin\phi,\; v_b\cos\phi \rangle, \qquad \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{ground}} = \langle v_r - v_b\sin\phi,\; v_b\cos\phi \rangle.
\]
Following a true due-North ground track means TWO conditions must hold:
1. Zero sideways (East–West) velocity: \(v_r - v_b\sin\phi = 0 \implies \sin\phi = \frac{v_r}{v_b}\).
2. Positive forward (North) progress: \(v_{\mathrm{ground}} = v_b\cos\phi > 0\).

Plugging in \(v_r = 6\) and \(v_b = 10\) gives \(\sin\phi = \frac{6}{10} = 0.6\), so \(\phi = \arcsin(0.6) \approx 36.9^\circ\) West of North—a compass heading of \(360^\circ - 36.9^\circ = 323.1^\circ\) (\(\text{N } 36.9^\circ \text{ W}\)). Using \(\cos\phi = \sqrt{1 - \sin^2\phi} = 0.8\), the resulting northward ground speed is
\[
v_{\mathrm{ground}} = v_b\cos\phi = \sqrt{v_b^2 - v_r^2} = \sqrt{10^2 - 6^2} = 8\text{ km/h}.
\]
Notice that the vectors \(\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{boat}}\), \(\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{river}}\), and \(\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{ground}}\) form a right triangle whose HYPOTENUSE is the craft's own speed \(v_b\), not the ground speed!

Now look closely at \(\sin\phi = \frac{v_r}{v_b}\). Because \(\sin\phi\) can never exceed \(1\), what happens if the cross-current speed \(v_r\) equals or exceeds the boat's maximum speed \(v_b\)?
- If \(v_r = v_b\), then \(\sin\phi = 1 \implies \phi = 90^\circ\): the boat must aim straight West just to hold its position against the current, giving \(v_{\mathrm{ground}} = 0\) (no northward progress).
- If \(v_r > v_b\), then \(\frac{v_r}{v_b} > 1\), so \(\sin\phi = \frac{v_r}{v_b}\) has NO real solution at all: even pointing straight upstream, the current overpowers the boat and sweeps it East.

The same logic works for ANY desired ground track—not just due North! Resolve the wind or current into two components: \(w_\perp\) perpendicular to the desired track (the crosswind) and \(w_\parallel\) along the desired track (positive for a tailwind, negative for a headwind). To cancel \(w_\perp\), the craft must dedicate a perpendicular component \(-w_\perp\), which is possible only if \(|w_\perp| \le v_c\). What remains of the craft's speed for forward motion along the track is \(\sqrt{v_c^2 - w_\perp^2}\), so the forward ground speed along the desired track is
\[
v_{\mathrm{ground}} = \sqrt{v_c^2 - w_\perp^2} + w_\parallel,
\]
and positive progress along the intended track requires BOTH \(|w_\perp| \le v_c\) AND \(\sqrt{v_c^2 - w_\perp^2} + w_\parallel > 0\).

[CALLOUT]
id: drift-correction
label: Method
title: Inverse drift correction (crabbing into a crosswind or current)
tone: key

To steer a craft of speed \(v_c\) along a prescribed ground track in a medium with cross-track component \(w_\perp\) and along-track component \(w_\parallel\):

(1) Cancel the cross-track component by choosing the crab angle \(\phi\) (into the crosswind/current) satisfying \(\sin\phi = \frac{|w_\perp|}{v_c}\).
(2) The resulting ground speed along the desired track is \(v_{\mathrm{ground}} = v_c\cos\phi + w_\parallel = \sqrt{v_c^2 - w_\perp^2} + w_\parallel\).
(3) Feasibility test: no forward solution exists if \(|w_\perp| > v_c\) or if \(\sqrt{v_c^2 - w_\perp^2} + w_\parallel \le 0\).
[/CALLOUT]

=== SECTION 3: Relative velocity between two moving objects ===

So far we combined a craft's velocity with the velocity of its medium. Now ask a closely related question: suppose two independent objects \(A\) and \(B\) move with constant ground velocities \(\mathbf{v}_A\) and \(\mathbf{v}_B\). How fast and in what direction does \(A\) appear to move as seen by an observer riding on \(B\)?

Let \(\mathbf{r}_A(0)\) and \(\mathbf{r}_B(0)\) be their starting position vectors. After elapsed time \(t > 0\), their positions are \(\mathbf{r}_A(t) = \mathbf{r}_A(0) + t\mathbf{v}_A\) and \(\mathbf{r}_B(t) = \mathbf{r}_B(0) + t\mathbf{v}_B\). The displacement vector pointing FROM observer \(B\) TO target \(A\) is
\[
\mathbf{r}_A(t) - \mathbf{r}_B(t) = \bigl(\mathbf{r}_A(0) - \mathbf{r}_B(0)\bigr) + t(\mathbf{v}_A - \mathbf{v}_B).
\]
Thus, per unit time, \(A\)'s position relative to \(B\) changes by the vector \(\mathbf{v}_A - \mathbf{v}_B\). We call this the relative velocity of \(A\) with respect to \(B\), written \(\mathbf{v}_{A/B}\):
\[
\mathbf{v}_{A/B} = \mathbf{v}_A - \mathbf{v}_B.
\]

[CALLOUT]
id: rel-vel
label: Formula
title: Relative velocity of \(A\) with respect to \(B\)
tone: key

If objects \(A\) and \(B\) move with velocities \(\mathbf{v}_A\) and \(\mathbf{v}_B\) relative to a common reference frame (such as the ground), then the velocity of \(A\) as observed by \(B\) is
\[
\mathbf{v}_{A/B} = \mathbf{v}_A - \mathbf{v}_B.
\]
Reversing observer and target reverses the vector: \(\mathbf{v}_{B/A} = \mathbf{v}_B - \mathbf{v}_A = -\mathbf{v}_{A/B}\). Notice also how [REF: vel-comp | velocity composition] is simply the rearrangement \(\mathbf{v}_{A/\text{ground}} = \mathbf{v}_{A/\text{medium}} + \mathbf{v}_{\text{medium}/\text{ground}}\)!
[/CALLOUT]

Always check the subtraction order with a quick mental test: subtract the OBSERVER's velocity. If you drive East at \(60\text{ km/h}\) (\(\mathbf{v}_B = \langle 60, 0 \rangle\)) and a faster car overtakes you heading East at \(80\text{ km/h}\) (\(\mathbf{v}_A = \langle 80, 0 \rangle\)), it moves ahead of you at \(\mathbf{v}_{A/B} = \langle 80, 0 \rangle - \langle 60, 0 \rangle = \langle +20, 0 \rangle\text{ km/h}\), while a roadside sign (\(\mathbf{v}_S = \mathbf{0}\)) rushes past you toward the West at \(\mathbf{0} - \langle 60, 0 \rangle = \langle -60, 0 \rangle\text{ km/h}\).

For a two-dimensional worked example, suppose Ship \(A\) sails due North at \(12\text{ km/h}\) (\(\mathbf{v}_A = \langle 0, 12 \rangle\)) while Ship \(B\) sails due East at \(16\text{ km/h}\) (\(\mathbf{v}_B = \langle 16, 0 \rangle\)). By [REF: rel-vel | the relative velocity formula], the velocity of Ship \(A\) as observed from Ship \(B\) is
\[
\mathbf{v}_{A/B} = \mathbf{v}_A - \mathbf{v}_B = \langle 0, 12 \rangle - \langle 16, 0 \rangle = \langle -16, 12 \rangle\text{ km/h}.
\]
Its relative speed is \(\|\mathbf{v}_{A/B}\| = \sqrt{(-16)^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{400} = 20\text{ km/h}\). Because \(-16 < 0\) and \(12 > 0\) (Quadrant II), its standard angle is \(\theta = \arctan\left(-\frac{12}{16}\right) + 180^\circ \approx -36.9^\circ + 180^\circ = 143.1^\circ\), which corresponds to compass bearing \(306.9^\circ\) (\(\text{N } 53.1^\circ \text{ W}\)). Conversely, from Ship \(A\)'s bridge, Ship \(B\) appears to move at \(\mathbf{v}_{B/A} = \langle 16, -12 \rangle\text{ km/h}\)—the same speed \(20\text{ km/h}\) on the opposite bearing \(126.9^\circ\) (\(\text{S } 53.1^\circ \text{ E}\)).

Inverse drift correction | Cancel \(w_\perp\): \(\sin\phi = \frac{\lvert w_\perp \rvert}{v_c}\) | Required heading angle \(\phi\) and resulting ground speed along track

[/TABLE]
