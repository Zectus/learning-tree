=== SECTION 1: A four-step diagnostic workflow for hybrid inequalities ===

Real problems rarely arrive labeled "absolute value," "rational," or "radical." An unfamiliar inequality such as \(\dfrac{|x - 2|}{x^2 - 1} \ge 1\), \(\sqrt{x^2 - 4x + 4} < 3 - x\), or \(\dfrac{|x| - 2}{\sqrt{x + 4} - 1} \le 0\) mixes several structures at once. If you jump straight into expanding or squaring, you create needless case splits and invite sign or domain errors.

Instead, pause for ten seconds and run a disciplined four-step diagnostic pass before writing any long algebra.

[CALLOUT]
id: four-step-workflow
label: Workflow
title: Four-step strategy for mixed inequalities
tone: key

(1) Fix and intersect all domain constraints first: require every even radicand \(\ge 0\) and every denominator \(\ne 0\). Check whether the domain itself already eliminates part or all of the inequality.
(2) Simplify structural identities before branching: replace \(\sqrt{u^2}\) with \(|u|\) (never with \(u\)), spot sign-definite quadratics (\(\Delta < 0\)), and identify strictly positive factors or terms that cannot change sign.
(3) Choose the lowest-branching method: use sign pruning or a domain-guarded sign chart for products and quotients, direct equivalences for single absolute values or radicals, substitution for repeated structures, and case splitting only as a last resort.
(4) Audit every boundary point: verify all numerator zeros, isolated zeros of non-negative factors, domain endpoints, and excluded denominator zeros against the ORIGINAL inequality.
[/CALLOUT]

Watch how [REF: four-step-workflow.1 | Step 1] and [REF: four-step-workflow.2 | Step 2] can collapse a scary-looking inequality in two lines.

Example 1 (domain and sign-definite pruning):
- Solve \(\sqrt{3 - x}\,(x^2 - 2x + 3) \le 0\).
  Domain: \(x \le 3\). Complete the square on the quadratic factor: \(x^2 - 2x + 3 = (x - 1)^2 + 2 \ge 2 > 0\) for EVERY \(x \in \mathbb{R}\). On the domain \((-\infty, 3]\), \(\sqrt{3 - x} \ge 0\) as well. Thus the product is \(\ge 0\) everywhere on its domain, so it can satisfy \(\le 0\) ONLY when it equals \(0\), which happens solely at \(x = 3\). Solution set: \(\{3\}\).
- Solve \(\sqrt{x - 5} + |x - 1| < 3\).
  Domain: \(x \ge 5\). On \(x \ge 5\), we automatically have \(|x - 1| = x - 1 \ge 4\) and \(\sqrt{x - 5} \ge 0\), so the left-hand side is at least \(4\) everywhere on its domain and can never be less than \(3\). Solution set: \(\emptyset\)—zero squaring, zero case splitting!

=== SECTION 2: Hidden absolute values and rational-absolute hybrids ===

One of the most frequent traps in hybrid inequalities is an even-power radical hiding an absolute value. Whenever a quadratic radicand is a perfect square, apply the exact identity \(\sqrt{u^2} = |u|\) BEFORE doing anything else.

Example 2: Solve \(\sqrt{x^2 - 4x + 4} < 3 - x\).
Step 1: The radicand factors as \((x - 2)^2 \ge 0\), so the domain is all of \(\mathbb{R}\).
Step 2: Apply \(\sqrt{(x - 2)^2} = |x - 2|\). The inequality transforms immediately into a variable-bound absolute value inequality:
\[
|x - 2| < 3 - x.
\]
Step 3: Use the direct equivalence \(|A| < B \iff -B < A < B\) (remembering that this automatically enforces \(B > 0\)!):
\[
-(3 - x) < x - 2 < 3 - x \iff x - 3 < x - 2 \quad \text{and} \quad x - 2 < 3 - x.
\]
The left inequality simplifies to \(-3 < -2\), which is true for every \(x \in \mathbb{R}\). The right inequality simplifies to \(2x < 5 \iff x < \frac{5}{2}\).
Step 4: Solution set: \(\left(-\infty, \frac{5}{2}\right)\).

The graph below confirms why replacing \(\sqrt{(x - 2)^2}\) by \(x - 2\) would have failed catastrophically: the curve \(y = \sqrt{x^2 - 4x + 4}\) is a V-shaped absolute value graph that stays non-negative for \(x < 2\), meeting the line \(y = 3 - x\) once at \(\left(\frac{5}{2}, \frac{1}{2}\right)\) and remaining strictly underneath it for every \(x < \frac{5}{2}\).

Now watch how sign pruning streamlines a rational-absolute inequality.

Example 3: Solve \(\dfrac{|x - 2|}{x^2 - 1} \ge 1\).
Step 1: Domain \(D = \mathbb{R} \setminus \{-1, 1\}\).
Step 2: Observe the sign of the numerator: \(|x - 2| \ge 0\) everywhere! Therefore, whenever the denominator \(x^2 - 1\) is NEGATIVE—that is, on \((-1, 1)\)—the quotient \(\dfrac{|x - 2|}{x^2 - 1}\) is \(\le 0\), so it can NEVER be \(\ge 1\). That eliminates \((-1, 1)\) immediately!
Step 3: On the remaining region \(x^2 - 1 > 0\) (meaning \(x < -1\) or \(x > 1\)), multiplying both sides by the strictly positive denominator \(x^2 - 1\) preserves the inequality:
\[
|x - 2| \ge x^2 - 1.
\]
Apply the variable-bound equivalence \(|A| \ge B \iff A \ge B \text{ or } A \le -B\):
- Branch A: \(x - 2 \ge x^2 - 1 \iff x^2 - x + 1 \le 0\). Check the discriminant: \(\Delta = (-1)^2 - 4(1)(1) = -3 < 0\) with leading coefficient \(1 > 0\), so \(x^2 - x + 1 > 0\) everywhere. Branch A yields \(\emptyset\).
- Branch B: \(x - 2 \le -(x^2 - 1) \iff x^2 + x - 3 \le 0\). By the quadratic formula, the roots are \(\dfrac{-1 \pm \sqrt{13}}{2}\) (approximately \(-2.303\) and \(1.303\)), so Branch B gives \(\left[\dfrac{-1 - \sqrt{13}}{2}, \dfrac{-1 + \sqrt{13}}{2}\right]\).
Step 4: Intersect Branch B with the positive-denominator region \((-\infty, -1) \cup (1, \infty)\):
\[
\left[\frac{-1 - \sqrt{13}}{2}, -1\right) \cup \left(1, \frac{-1 + \sqrt{13}}{2}\right].
\]
Both excluded denominator points \(x = \pm 1\) carry open parentheses, while the two roots where equality holds carry closed brackets.

=== SECTION 3: Quotients mixing absolute values and radicals ===

When a single fraction compares to \(0\) and mixes an absolute value in one part with a radical in the other, resist the temptation to break into cases on the sign inside the absolute value! A single domain-guarded sign chart handles the entire problem at once, because we only need the zeros of the numerator and denominator and the sign of each expression between them.

Example 4: Solve \(\dfrac{|x| - 2}{\sqrt{x + 4} - 1} \le 0\).
Step 1 (Domain): We need \(x + 4 \ge 0\) (so \(x \ge -4\)) and \(\sqrt{x + 4} - 1 \ne 0 \iff x + 4 \ne 1 \iff x \ne -3\). Thus
\[
D = [-4, -3) \cup (-3, \infty).
\]
Step 2 (Critical points inside \(D\)):
- Numerator zeros: \(|x| - 2 = 0 \iff x = -2\) or \(x = 2\). Both lie in \(D\) and will be INCLUDED because the inequality is \(\le 0\).
- Denominator zero: \(x = -3\) (EXCLUDED).

Step 3 (Domain-guarded sign chart on \([-4, \infty)\)):

[TABLE]
Interval or point | \(\lvert x \rvert - 2\) | \(\sqrt{x + 4} - 1\) | Quotient | Satisfies \(\le 0\)?
\([-4, -3)\) | \(+\) | \(-\) | \(-\) | Yes
\(x = -3\) | \(1\) | \(0\) | undefined | No
\((-3, -2)\) | \(+\) | \(+\) | \(+\) | No
\(x = -2\) | \(0\) | \(\sqrt{2} - 1\) | \(0\) | Yes
\((-2, 2)\) | \(-\) | \(+\) | \(-\) | Yes
\(x = 2\) | \(0\) | \(\sqrt{6} - 1\) | \(0\) | Yes
\((2, \infty)\) | \(+\) | \(+\) | \(+\) | No
[/TABLE]

Step 4 (Assemble and check endpoints): Reading off the "Yes" rows produces
\[
[-4, -3) \cup [-2, 2].
\]
Check the left domain endpoint \(x = -4\) explicitly: \(\dfrac{|-4| - 2}{\sqrt{0} - 1} = \dfrac{2}{-1} = -2 \le 0\), so \(-4\) definitely belongs in the solution set.

The graph below shows both branches of \(y = \dfrac{|x| - 2}{\sqrt{x + 4} - 1}\) separated by the vertical asymptote at \(x = -3\). Notice how the left branch starts at \((-4, -2)\) and stays negative up to \(x = -3\), while the right branch drops below the horizontal axis precisely on \([-2, 2]\).

Before picking a method on any unfamiliar hybrid inequality, use this quick decision guide to choose the path with the fewest branches:

[TABLE]
Feature spotted in the inequality | Best immediate action
\(\sqrt{a x^2 + b x + c}\) where radicand is a perfect square \(u^2\) | Replace immediately with \(\lvert u \rvert\)
Sign-definite quadratic (\(\Delta < 0\)) or strictly positive sum | Note its constant sign and factor it out of the sign analysis
Single quotient or product compared with \(0\) | Fix domain \(D\), find all zeros, run one domain-guarded sign chart
Non-negative numerator over variable denominator compared with a positive constant | Prune the negative-denominator region immediately, then multiply through
Domain forces a term above or below the bound | Conclude \(\emptyset\) or a single boundary point without expanding
[/TABLE]
