=== SECTION 1: Outer absolute value: \(y = |f(x)|\) ===

The affine transformations \(a \cdot f(b(x - h)) + k\) shift, reflect, and scale a graph smoothly without introducing new corners or erasing parts of the curve. Placing an absolute value around a function—either on the outside as \(y = |f(x)|\) or on the inside as \(y = f(|x|)\)—behaves completely differently: it folds or mirrors parts of the graph nonlinearly. Fortunately, the piecewise definition of absolute value tells us exactly what each operation does, with zero guesswork.

Start with the outer absolute value \(y = |f(x)|\). Applying the definition of absolute value to the output \(f(x)\) gives the exact piecewise bridge:
\[
|f(x)| = \begin{cases}
f(x), & \text{where } f(x) \ge 0, \\
-f(x), & \text{where } f(x) < 0.
\end{cases}
\]
Read those two branches geometrically:
- Wherever the graph of \(y = f(x)\) lies on or above the \(x\)-axis (\(f(x) \ge 0\)), leave it completely untouched.
- Wherever the graph of \(y = f(x)\) dips strictly below the \(x\)-axis (\(f(x) < 0\)), replace \(y\) by \(-y\), reflecting that negative piece upward across the \(x\)-axis.

[CALLOUT]
id: outer-abs
label: Rule
title: Outer absolute-value transformation \(y = |f(x)|\)
tone: key

To obtain the graph of \(y = |f(x)|\) from the graph of \(y = f(x)\):

(1) Keep all points with \(f(x) \ge 0\) unchanged.
(2) Reflect all points with \(f(x) < 0\) upward across the \(x\)-axis: \((x, y) \mapsto (x, -y)\).
(3) Domain is unchanged: \(\operatorname{Dom}(|f|) = \operatorname{Dom}(f)\). All outputs become nonnegative (\(\operatorname{Range}(|f|) \subseteq [0, \infty)\)).
(4) At every \(x\)-intercept where \(y = f(x)\) crosses the \(x\)-axis at a nonzero angle, the upward fold creates a sharp corner pointing down to the \(x\)-axis.
[/CALLOUT]

Watch [REF: outer-abs | this folding rule] act on the shifted parabola \(f(x) = x^2 - 4\), which dips below the \(x\)-axis on \((-2, 2)\) down to its vertex \((0, -4)\). Under \(y = |x^2 - 4|\), the outer arms on \((-\infty, -2]\) and \([2, \infty)\) stay unchanged, while the negative trough between \(-2\) and \(2\) flips upward into a hill peaking at \((0, 4)\), creating two sharp corners at \((-2, 0)\) and \((2, 0)\).

Now apply [REF: outer-abs | the same rule] to a transformed reciprocal function with asymptotes:
\[
g(x) = \left|\frac{1}{x - 1} - 2\right|.
\]
Start with the inside function \(h(x) = \frac{1}{x - 1} - 2\), which has vertical asymptote \(x = 1\), horizontal asymptote \(y = -2\), and \(x\)-intercept at \(x = \frac{3}{2}\) (where \(\frac{1}{x - 1} = 2\)).
- For \(1 < x \le \frac{3}{2}\), \(h(x) \ge 0\), so that piece stays unchanged.
- For \(x < 1\) and for \(x > \frac{3}{2}\), \(h(x) < 0\), so both of those pieces reflect upward across the \(x\)-axis! In particular, the horizontal asymptote \(y = -2\) reflects upward to \(y = 2\), while the vertical asymptote stays at \(x = 1\).
- The domain stays \((-\infty, 1) \cup (1, \infty)\), and because \(h(x)\) reaches \(0\) at \(x = \frac{3}{2}\) and grows unboundedly near \(x = 1\), the range of \(g(x) = |h(x)|\) is \([0, \infty)\). (Notice that \(y = 2\) IS attained in the range on the right-hand positive piece, at \(x = \frac{5}{4}\) where \(h(5/4) = 4 - 2 = 2\)!)

=== SECTION 2: Inner absolute value: \(y = f(|x|)\) ===

Now put the absolute-value bars INSIDE the parentheses to form \(y = f(|x|)\). Again, rewrite it directly using the piecewise definition of \(|x|\):
\[
f(|x|) = \begin{cases}
f(x), & x \ge 0, \\
f(-x), & x < 0.
\end{cases}
\]
Watch what happens to the original left side of \(y = f(x)\) (where \(x < 0\)): it NEVER gets used! When you plug a negative number like \(x = -3\) into \(f(|x|)\), the absolute value strips off the minus sign first, evaluating \(f(3)\) instead of \(f(-3)\).

[CALLOUT]
id: inner-abs
label: Rule
title: Inner absolute-value transformation \(y = f(|x|)\)
tone: key

To obtain the graph of \(y = f(|x|)\) from the graph of \(y = f(x)\):

(1) Discard the entire portion of the graph to the left of the \(y\)-axis (\(x < 0\)).
(2) Keep the right-hand portion (\(x \ge 0\)) unchanged.
(3) Reflect that right-hand portion across the \(y\)-axis onto the left side (\(x < 0\)).
(4) The resulting function \(g(x) = f(|x|)\) is ALWAYS even, because \(g(-x) = f(|-x|) = f(|x|) = g(x)\), and its domain is symmetric about \(0\): \(x \in \operatorname{Dom}(g) \iff |x| \in \operatorname{Dom}(f)\).
[/CALLOUT]

Look at the dramatic difference between [REF: outer-abs | \(|f(x)|\)] and [REF: inner-abs | \(f(|x|)\)] on the shifted parabola \(f(x) = (x - 1)^2 - 4\), which has vertex \((1, -4)\) and \(y\)-intercept \((0, -3)\):
- To graph \(y = f(|x|) = (|x| - 1)^2 - 4\), discard \(x < 0\) (which throws away the \(x\)-intercept at \((-1, 0)\)), keep the \(x \ge 0\) branch (passing through \((0, -3)\), vertex \((1, -4)\), and \((3, 0)\)), and mirror that right branch across the \(y\)-axis! The result is a W-shaped even curve with TWO global minima at \((-1, -4)\) and \((1, -4)\), a sharp local-maximum corner at \((0, -3)\), and \(x\)-intercepts at \((-3, 0)\) and \((3, 0)\).

Notice how [REF: inner-abs.4 | the domain rule] can either shrink OR expand the domain of \(f\):
- If \(f(x) = \sqrt{x - 2}\) has domain \([2, \infty)\), then \(f(|x|) = \sqrt{|x| - 2}\) requires \(|x| \ge 2\), giving domain \((-\infty, -2] \cup [2, \infty)\)—mirroring the right branch creates a brand-new left branch!
- If \(f\) is defined only on \([-5, 2]\), then \(f(|x|)\) discards \([-5, 0)\), keeps \([0, 2]\), and mirrors it to \([-2, 2]\).

=== SECTION 3: Combining both operations: \(|f(|x|)|\) and order with shifts ===

What happens when we apply BOTH absolute values to form \(y = |f(|x|)|\)? Start from the inside out:
1. First form \(g(x) = f(|x|)\) using [REF: inner-abs | the inner rule]: keep \(x \ge 0\) and mirror it across the \(y\)-axis.
2. Then form \(|g(x)| = |f(|x|)|\) using [REF: outer-abs | the outer rule]: fold every negative portion of \(g\) upward across the \(x\)-axis.

Wait: does the order between outer and inner absolute value matter? Check! For any \(x\):
- Inner first, then outer: \(|f(|x|)|\).
- Outer first (define \(h(x) = |f(x)|\)), then inner: \(h(|x|) = |f(|x|)|\).
They produce the exact same expression! Outer and inner absolute values commute because one acts purely on outputs while the other acts purely on inputs.

However, absolute values do NOT commute with shifts or reflections on the same axis! Compare two deceptively similar expressions built from \(f(x) = x^2\):
- \(p(x) = |x^2 - 2|\): first shift \(x^2\) down \(2\) (dropping the vertex to \((0, -2)\)), THEN take the outer absolute value (folding the negative trough upward to a peak at \((0, 2)\)). Its range is \([0, \infty)\).
- \(q(x) = |x^2| - 2 = x^2 - 2\): first take the outer absolute value (which leaves \(x^2 \ge 0\) unchanged), THEN shift down \(2\). Its range is \([-2, \infty)\).

Even on a function that already takes negative values, such as \(f(x) = x - 1\), shifting before versus after \(|\cdot|\) produces completely different curves:
- \(|f(x) - 2| = |x - 3|\): vertex at \((3, 0)\), range \([0, \infty)\).
- \(|f(x)| - 2 = |x - 1| - 2\): vertex at \((1, -2)\), range \([-2, \infty)\).

Likewise horizontally, compare \(f(|x| - 1)\) with \(f(|x - 1|)\):
- In \(f(|x - 1|)\), the reflection symmetry axis moves from \(x = 0\) to \(x = 1\) (discard \(x < 1\), mirror \(x \ge 1\) across the vertical line \(x = 1\)).
- In \(f(|x| - 1)\), first shift \(f\) right \(1\) to form \(h(x) = f(x - 1)\), and then apply the inner absolute value \(h(|x|)\), which mirrors across \(x = 0\)!

The table below contrasts all four core absolute-value operations on a general function \(f\).

[TABLE]
Expression | Geometric action | Guaranteed symmetry | Effect on range
\(\lvert f(x) \rvert\) | Reflect \(f(x) < 0\) pieces upward across \(x\)-axis | None in general | Subset of \([0, \infty)\)
\(f(\lvert x \rvert)\) | Discard \(x < 0\); mirror \(x \ge 0\) across \(y\)-axis | Always even | Range of \(f\) restricted to \(x \ge 0\)
\(\lvert f(\lvert x \rvert) \rvert\) | Mirror \(x \ge 0\) across \(y\)-axis, then fold negatives upward | Always even | Nonnegative magnitudes of \(f\) on \(x \ge 0\)
\(\lvert f(x) \rvert + k\) | Fold negatives upward first, then shift vertically by \(k\) | None in general | Subset of \([k, \infty)\)
[/TABLE]

=== SECTION 4: Analyzing abstract graphs under absolute-value operations ===

When \(y = f(x)\) is given only as a sketch or by key feature points, apply [REF: outer-abs | the outer rule] and [REF: inner-abs | the inner rule] point by point, paying special attention to intercepts and extrema:
- For \(|f(x)|\): every \(x\)-intercept of \(f\) becomes a global minimum of \(0\); any negative local minimum \((c, -m)\) with \(m > 0\) flips up into a local maximum \((c, m)\).
- For \(f(|x|)\): every point \((a, b)\) of \(f\) with \(a > 0\) generates a twin point \((-a, b)\); points of \(f\) with \(a < 0\) disappear completely; the \(y\)-intercept \((0, f(0))\) stays fixed (often becoming a sharp corner if the curve arrives at \(x = 0\) with a nonzero slope).

Consider an unlabeled function \(f\) whose graph is the straight line segment from \((-2, 4)\) through \((0, 2)\) and \((2, 0)\) down to \((4, -2)\), i.e., \(f(x) = 2 - x\) on \([-2, 4]\):
- \(|f(x)|\) keeps \((-2, 4)\) to \((2, 0)\) and reflects \((2, 0)\) to \((4, -2)\) upward to \((4, 2)\), forming a V with vertex \((2, 0)\), domain \([-2, 4]\), and range \([0, 4]\).
- \(f(|x|)\) discards \([-2, 0)\), keeps \((0, 2)\) to \((4, -2)\), and mirrors it across the \(y\)-axis to \((-4, -2)\), forming a symmetric tent with peak \((0, 2)\), domain \([-4, 4]\), and range \([-2, 2]\).
