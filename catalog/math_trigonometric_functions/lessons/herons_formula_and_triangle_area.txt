=== SECTION 1: Finding triangle area directly from three side lengths ===

Suppose a land surveyor measures the three boundary fences of a triangular field—say \(a = 13\text{ m}\), \(b = 14\text{ m}\), and \(c = 15\text{ m}\)—without measuring a single angle. You ALREADY know one way to compute its area: use the Law of Cosines to find \(\cos C\), recover an approximate angle \(C\), and plug into the SAS area formula \(K = \frac{1}{2}ab\sin C\). Yet going through an approximate angle seems strangely indirect when the area depends purely on the three side lengths \(a, b, c\)!

Can we eliminate angle \(C\) algebraically once and for all to obtain a direct, exact formula for \(K\) purely in terms of \(a, b, c\)? Yes—and the derivation uses nothing beyond \(K = \frac{1}{2}ab\sin C\), the Pythagorean identity \(\sin^2 C = 1 - \cos^2 C\), the Law of Cosines, and difference-of-squares factoring!

Start with the SAS area formula and square both sides so \(\sin^2 C\) appears:
\[
K^2 = \frac{1}{4}a^2b^2\sin^2 C = \frac{1}{4}a^2b^2(1 - \cos^2 C) = \frac{1}{4}a^2b^2(1 + \cos C)(1 - \cos C).
\]
Now substitute the Law of Cosines \(\cos C = \frac{a^2 + b^2 - c^2}{2ab}\) into each factor separately. Look how both numerators collapse into perfect squares minus squares:
\[
1 + \cos C = 1 + \frac{a^2 + b^2 - c^2}{2ab} = \frac{a^2 + 2ab + b^2 - c^2}{2ab} = \frac{(a + b)^2 - c^2}{2ab} = \frac{(a + b + c)(a + b - c)}{2ab},
\]
\[
1 - \cos C = 1 - \frac{a^2 + b^2 - c^2}{2ab} = \frac{2ab - a^2 - b^2 + c^2}{2ab} = \frac{c^2 - (a - b)^2}{2ab} = \frac{(c - a + b)(c + a - b)}{2ab}.
\]
Multiply those two fractions back into the expression for \(K^2\). Notice that \((2ab)(2ab) = 4a^2b^2\) cancels the outside \(a^2b^2\) completely, leaving \(\frac{1}{4} \cdot \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{16}\):
\[
K^2 = \frac{1}{16}(a + b + c)(b + c - a)(a + c - b)(a + b - c).
\]
That formula is already symmetric in \(a, b, c\), and one final substitution makes it astonishingly clean. Define the semiperimeter \(s\) (half the perimeter):
\[
s = \frac{a + b + c}{2} \iff a + b + c = 2s.
\]
Subtracting \(2a\), \(2b\), and \(2c\) in turn from \(a + b + c = 2s\) rewrites the remaining three factors:
\[
b + c - a = 2s - 2a = 2(s - a), \qquad a + c - b = 2(s - b), \qquad a + b - c = 2(s - c).
\]
Substituting all four factors into \(K^2\), the four factors of \(2\) multiply to \(2^4 = 16\) and cancel \(\frac{1}{16}\) completely:
\[
K^2 = \frac{1}{16} \cdot 2s \cdot 2(s - a) \cdot 2(s - b) \cdot 2(s - c) = s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c).
\]
Taking the positive square root produces Heron's formula.

[CALLOUT]
id: herons-formula
label: Theorem
title: Heron's Formula for Triangle Area
tone: key

For any triangle with side lengths \(a, b, c\) and semiperimeter \(s = \dfrac{a + b + c}{2}\), the exact area \(K\) is
\[
K = \sqrt{s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)}.
\]
[/CALLOUT]

=== SECTION 2: Applying Heron's formula and the triangle inequality ===

Test [REF: herons-formula | Heron's formula] on our opening field with sides \(a = 13\text{ m}\), \(b = 14\text{ m}\), and \(c = 15\text{ m}\).

1. Compute the semiperimeter: \(s = \frac{13 + 14 + 15}{2} = \frac{42}{2} = 21\text{ m}\).
2. Compute the three differences:
\[
s - a = 21 - 13 = 8, \qquad s - b = 21 - 14 = 7, \qquad s - c = 21 - 15 = 6.
\]
(Quick arithmetic check: \((s - a) + (s - b) + (s - c)\) must ALWAYS equal \(s\)! Here \(8 + 7 + 6 = 21 = s\).)
3. Factor BEFORE multiplying so the square root simplifies effortlessly:
\[
K = \sqrt{21 \cdot 8 \cdot 7 \cdot 6} = \sqrt{(3 \cdot 7) \cdot (2^3) \cdot 7 \cdot (2 \cdot 3)} = \sqrt{2^4 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 7^2} = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 7 = 84\text{ m}^2.
\]
Notice the exact geometry of this classic triangle below: placed on base \(b = 14\), vertex \(B\) lands at \((5, 12)\) with height \(12\) (splitting the base into \(5\) and \(9\), forming \(5\)-\(12\)-\(13\) and \(9\)-\(12\)-\(15\) right triangles!), confirming \(\frac{1}{2}(14)(12) = 84\text{ m}^2\).

Look closely at the three difference factors \(s - a = \frac{b + c - a}{2}\), \(s - b = \frac{a + c - b}{2}\), and \(s - c = \frac{a + b - c}{2}\). The triangle inequality says \(b + c > a\), \(a + c > b\), and \(a + b > c\)—which means ALL THREE factors \(s - a\), \(s - b\), and \(s - c\) are strictly positive if and only if \(a, b, c\) form a valid non-degenerate triangle!
- If the two shorter sides equal the longest side (say \(3, 4, 7\)), then \(s = 7\) and \(s - 7 = 0\), yielding \(K = 0\) (a flattened line segment of zero area).
- If the two shorter sides sum to LESS than the longest side (say \(3, 4, 9\)), then \(s = 8\) and \(s - 9 = -1 < 0\), while the other three factors are positive, making the product inside the square root NEGATIVE—an immediate algebraic alarm that no triangle exists!

[CALLOUT]
id: heron-tip
label: Practical tip
title: Simplify radicals by factoring before multiplying
tone: note

In Heron's formula, multiplying four large numbers together first (such as \(21 \cdot 8 \cdot 7 \cdot 6 = 7056\)) makes spotting perfect squares much harder. Keep \(s\), \(s - a\), \(s - b\), and \(s - c\) separated, prime-factor each one, and pull pairs out of the square root immediately. Also check \((s - a) + (s - b) + (s - c) = s\) to catch arithmetic slips before taking the root.
[/CALLOUT]

=== SECTION 3: Measuring irregular polygonal land parcels ===

Surveyors frequently measure the area of an irregular four-sided or five-sided lot using only a tape measure (no angle instruments needed!): measure all outer boundary sides plus enough interior diagonals to partition the polygon into triangles, then apply [REF: herons-formula | Heron's formula] to each triangle and sum the areas.

Consider the four-sided parcel \(ABCD\) shown below, with outer sides \(AB = 13\text{ m}\), \(AD = 20\text{ m}\), \(BC = 10\text{ m}\), \(CD = 17\text{ m}\), and measured diagonal \(BD = 21\text{ m}\).

Compute each triangle's area separately using [REF: heron-tip | our factoring strategy]:
- Upper triangle \(\triangle ABD\) (\(13, 20, 21\)): \(s_1 = \frac{13 + 20 + 21}{2} = 27\). Differences: \(14, 7, 6\) (check: \(14 + 7 + 6 = 27\)).
\[
K_1 = \sqrt{27 \cdot 14 \cdot 7 \cdot 6} = \sqrt{(3^3) \cdot (2 \cdot 7) \cdot 7 \cdot (2 \cdot 3)} = \sqrt{2^2 \cdot 3^4 \cdot 7^2} = 2 \cdot 9 \cdot 7 = 126\text{ m}^2.
\]
- Lower triangle \(\triangle BCD\) (\(10, 17, 21\)): \(s_2 = \frac{10 + 17 + 21}{2} = 24\). Differences: \(14, 7, 3\) (check: \(14 + 7 + 3 = 24\)).
\[
K_2 = \sqrt{24 \cdot 14 \cdot 7 \cdot 3} = \sqrt{(2^3 \cdot 3) \cdot (2 \cdot 7) \cdot 7 \cdot 3} = \sqrt{2^4 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 7^2} = 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 7 = 84\text{ m}^2.
\]
- Total parcel area: \(K = 126 + 84 = 210\text{ m}^2\), obtained cleanly without measuring a single angle!
